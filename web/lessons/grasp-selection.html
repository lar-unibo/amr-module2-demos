A novel object

There is no model, so no grasp planned in advance: the grasp is chosen on the point cloud itself. The workspace shows a cross-section of a table scene. Depth cameras above it measure points along their rays (top right). The points on the table are removed, which leaves the object's cloud (left). The pipeline of the slide:

normals and curvature → antipodal candidates → evaluate and select → grasp and pregrasp pose.

Normals by local PCA

The k nearest points p_j of a point p_i, with centroid \bar p, give

\mathrm{Cov}_i=\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^{k}(p_j-\bar p)(p_j-\bar p)^T,

with eigenvalues \lambda_0\leq\lambda_1. The neighbours spread along the surface, so v_0, the direction of smallest spread, is the normal. Drag the ring along the cloud: the card on the right shows its neighbours and the ellipse of \mathrm{Cov}_i.

Try: set the depth noise to 1.5 mm. With k=3 the normals are noisy (median error 21°, against 4° with k=15). With k=40 the neighbourhoods reach around the corners and bend the normals of the sides. The antipodal pairs halve, and the best grasp is about 5° off.

Which way is out?

PCA gives the normal only up to its sign, \pm v_0. The camera sees the object from outside, so each normal is flipped towards the camera that measured the point:

n_i\cdot(p_{cam}-p_i)>0.

Try: untick orient the normals. About half of them now point inside the object, and the antipodal pairs drop from 439 to 72.

Surface variation

The same eigenvalues measure how far the neighbours are from a straight line (a plane, in 3-D):

\sigma_i=\frac{\lambda_0}{\lambda_0+\lambda_1}\in\left[0,\tfrac12\right].

The colours of the cloud show it. On the sides of the box \sigma_i<0.01, at its corners about 0.17, on the rim of the cup about 0.2. The thin walls of the cup also look like edges, because the neighbours of a point include the other face of the wall, 6 mm away.

Antipodal grasps

A parallel-jaw gripper closes along u=(c_2-c_1)/\|c_2-c_1\|. The pair is accepted if u lies inside both friction cones (the outward normal n_i here; the contact frames of Part 1 use the inward one, -n_i):

\angle(u,-n_1)<\arctan\mu,\qquad \angle(-u,-n_2)<\arctan\mu,

and if it fits: \|c_2-c_1\|< the maximum opening. For two hard fingers in the plane this is exactly force closure (the pinch of demo 01).

Try: \mu=0.1,\,0.2,\,0.4,\,0.8 accept 109, 226, 439 and 823 pairs. A maximum opening of 5 cm finds no grasp on the 6 cm box.

Partial views

A camera sees only the side of the object facing it. Try: switch to one camera. The right side of the box is not measured, and neither is the bottom: no pair passes the test, for any of the three objects. Two cameras (fusion) see both sides.

Score, select, approach

For each antipodal pair the gripper approaches along z_g, perpendicular to u. Poses whose fingers, palm or wrist hit a measured point or the table are discarded, along the whole straight approach. The others get a cost; the cheapest one is drawn:

C=C_{\text{antipodal}}+\alpha_2\,C_{\text{reach}}+\alpha_3\,C_{\text{clear}}.

The pregrasp has the same orientation, d back along z_g: {}^NT_{pre}={}^NT_{grasp}\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-d\,e_z\\0&1\end{bmatrix}. From the pregrasp, a short straight line reaches the grasp.

Try: on the cup the best grasp takes one wall at the rim (0.65 cm across). Open the gripper to 12 cm: the whole cup, 9 cm across, becomes cheaper. Click the rows of the table to see the other candidates.

Slides and simulation

Lecture 3, slides 51–57 and 60, on a 2-D cross-section of the scene (x horizontal, z up, table at z = 0). In 2-D the covariance has two eigenvalues, so \sigma_i\in[0,\frac12] ([0,\frac13] in 3-D). Each camera has one row of 320 pixels and a 44° field of view, at (±16 cm, 42 cm). A pixel u with depth d gives p=d\,K^{-1}[u,\,1]^T, with Gaussian noise on d. There is no measurement where a ray meets the surface at a grazing angle (|\cos|<0.15), and 4% of the pixels drop out at random. Without orientation the sign of v_0 is drawn at random, as an eigen-solver gives no rule.

Every pair of points is tested; at most 500 of the most antipodal pairs are evaluated, each with the approaches from above or the side. The gripper (fingers 4.5 cm × 0.8 cm, palm as wide as the maximum opening) opens 2 cm wider than the pair. Space that no camera measured counts as free. Cost terms, each in [0, 1]: C_{\text{antipodal}}=\max(\theta_1,\theta_2)/\arctan\mu, C_{\text{reach}} = angle of z_g from straight down / 90°, and C_{\text{clear}}=1- clearance / 1 cm.
