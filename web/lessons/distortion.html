Geometric distortion

Real lenses do not follow the central-projection model exactly. The most important effect for robotics is geometric distortion: straight lines in the scene become curved in the image. It has two parts:

  • radial: points move along lines through the principal point, by an amount that grows with the distance r from it;
  • tangential (decentring): the lens is not centred over the sensor or not parallel to it, so the image looks slightly tilted or stretched.

The model

With five parameters D=(k_1,k_2,k_3,p_1,p_2), considered additional intrinsic parameters:

\begin{aligned} x_d &= x\,(1+k_1r^2+k_2r^4+k_3r^6) + \delta_x\\ y_d &= y\,(1+k_1r^2+k_2r^4+k_3r^6) + \delta_y \end{aligned}
\begin{aligned} \delta_x &= 2p_1xy + p_2(r^2+2x^2)\\ \delta_y &= p_1(r^2+2y^2) + 2p_2xy \end{aligned}

Here (x,y) are normalized image coordinates (image plane at f=1, centred on the principal point), as in OpenCV; the pixels then follow from \vK. Slide 28 writes the same formulas with (u,v); measured in pixels from the principal point, the coefficients are much smaller.

Try

  • barrel (k_1<0): the corners move inward, typical of wide-angle lenses. pincushion (k_1>0): outward, typical of telephoto lenses.
  • The arrows are nearly zero at the centre and largest at the corners: to leading order the radial displacement grows like r^3. With barrel, a corner moves by about 27 pixels, a point at half that distance from the centre by under 4.
  • tangential: the displacement is no longer along radial lines.
  • Show the corrected grid: inverting the model (by iteration) puts the points back on straight lines, to a fraction of a pixel.
