The problem

A known object, an unknown pose. The model points m_i are sampled on the CAD model, in the object frame (red, around the origin). The scene points s_i are measured, in the world frame (blue). Here the correspondences are known: m_i and s_i are the same physical point (dashed pairs):

T_{obj}\,m_i = s_i,\qquad i=1,\ldots,N.

The transform that aligns the model with the scene is the object pose T_{obj}. A grasp defined once on the model, {}^{obj}T_{grasp}, then follows: {}^{N}T_{grasp}=T_{obj}\,{}^{obj}T_{grasp}.

Each pair gives 3 equations. Without noise, 3 non-collinear pairs fix the 6 unknowns of T_{obj}. With noise the equations cannot all hold, so we solve them in the least-squares sense:

\min_{R\in SO(3),\,t}\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lVert R\,m_i+t-s_i\rVert^2 .

Try: press 3 pairs: three exact pairs, and the pose is recovered to machine precision.

The closed form, in five steps

The card on the right follows slide 44 with the current numbers:

  1. centroids \bar m, \bar s (the large rings) and centred points \tilde m_i=m_i-\bar m, \tilde s_i=s_i-\bar s;
  2. cross-covariance M=\sum_i \tilde s_i\,\tilde m_i^T;
  3. SVD M=U\Sigma V^T;
  4. R^*=U D V^T, with D=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,\det(UV^T));
  5. t^*=\bar s-R^*\bar m: the model centroid lands on the scene centroid.

The red rings are the aligned model R^*m_i+t^*, and the dashed red outline is the object at the estimated pose.

Try: raise the noise. The rings stay on the blue points on average, and the pose error grows slowly: with 30 pairs, 10 mm of noise gives about 3°. Add pairs to average the noise down.

Why D: the derivation (separate slide)

Expanding the cost, only one term depends on R:

\sum_i\lVert\tilde s_i-R\tilde m_i\rVert^2=\sum_i\lVert\tilde s_i\rVert^2+\sum_i\lVert\tilde m_i\rVert^2-2\,\mathrm{tr}(RM^T),

and \mathrm{tr}(RM^T)=\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma Q) with Q=U^TRV orthogonal. Its maximum is at Q=I, that is R=UV^T. But if \det(UV^T)=-1, that R is a reflection. The best Q with the right determinant is D: it flips the smallest singular value \sigma_3 and raises the cost by 4\sigma_3.

Try: press mirror: a 2 mm plate, 12 noisy pairs, and D switched off. Here \det(UV^T)=-1. The red frame R is left-handed and the object outline is mirrored, yet its residual is lower than that of any rotation (7.8 mm vs 8.2 mm). Switch D on: a rotation, 1.8° from the truth. Points that are nearly coplanar have a tiny \sigma_3, and the noise decides the sign of \det M.

Wrong pairs

Least squares trusts every pair. A few wrong correspondences (orange) pull the whole estimate.

Try: wrong pairs swaps 4 of the 30 pairs: the residual jumps from 3 to 25 mm and the rotation error quadruples. Real correspondences come from matching, so outliers are common. This is why the next steps reject pairs, and why RANSAC fits many 3-pair subsets and keeps the one with the most inliers (slide 45).

The model points are sampled on the surface of an L-shaped bracket, 16 × 10 × 12 cm, or of a 2 mm thick L-shaped plate. The scene points are the same points moved by the true pose, with Gaussian noise of the given σ on each coordinate. Wrong pairs exchange scene points cyclically among randomly chosen pairs. new noise draws another sample. Here the world frame is the camera frame (T_{cam}=I), so s_i=s_i^c.
