The depth is usually not known

The translation columns of \vL_p contain 1/Z, the depth of each point. A single camera does not measure it. Three options:

  1. a 3-D camera (stereo, time of flight, structured light): the true depths;
  2. a constant guess \hat Z for every point;
  3. an online estimate from the measured camera and image motion.

A wrong constant guess

The scene of slide 33: the true depth at the goal is Z=3 m. With \hat Z in place of Z, the commanded translation is roughly scaled by \hat Z/Z; near the goal, \Lhp\vL=\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac{\hat Z}{Z}\vI_3,\vI_3) exactly.

Try: \hat Z=3, then \hat Z=1: about three times slower. Then \hat Z=10: translation velocities about 10/3 times larger. All converge, and the final pose is just as accurate: a coarse depth perturbs the trajectory, not the result.

Estimating the depth online

Split \vL_p into its translation and rotation parts. With the camera velocity known and the image velocity measured, each point gives a linear equation in \phi=1/Z:

\underbrace{(\vL_\nu\,\nu_c)}_{\mathbf A}\;\phi=\underbrace{\dot{\vs}-\vL_\omega\,\omega_c}_{\mathbf b} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \phi=\frac{\mathbf A^{T}\mathbf b}{\mathbf A^{T}\mathbf A}.

Try: online estimate starting from \hat Z_0=1: after the first motion, the dashed estimates join the true depths (bottom-right plot), and the run behaves like the one with true depths.

The estimate needs translation: without \nu_c, \mathbf A=\mathbf 0 and the depth is unobservable (the estimate is then kept).
