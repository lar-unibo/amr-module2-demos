The global condition

With \mathcal V=\tfrac12\norm{\ve}^2 and \vv_c=-\lambda\Lhp\ve, \dot{\mathcal V}=-\lambda\,\ve^{T}\vL\Lhp\ve. Global asymptotic stability needs \vL\Lhp>0.

With four points this 8\times8 matrix has rank at most 6: the card on the right always shows two zero eigenvalues. Two directions of the error are invisible to the controller, so the global condition can never hold.

The error the controller acts on

The controller only uses \ve'=\Lhp\ve\in\R^6: \vv_c=-\lambda\ve'. With \Lhp constant,

\dot{\ve}'=\Lhp\dot{\ve}=\Lhp\vL\,\vv_c=-\lambda\,\Lhp\vL\,\ve',

and \Lhp\vL is only 6\times6: if it is positive definite, \ve'\to\mathbf 0, and near the goal also \ve\to\mathbf 0. Its smallest eigenvalue is plotted bottom right; with \vL(\vs) and true depths it is exactly 1.

Try: \vL(\vs^{*}) from 1 rad about z: the eigenvalue dips well below zero along the way, yet the run converges. The condition is sufficient, not necessary, and only local. From large tilt, \vL(\vs^{*}) fails: the points pass behind the camera.

A constant \hat Z only scales the translation part: at the goal, \Lhp\vL=\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac{\hat Z}{Z}\vI_3,\vI_3) exactly (slide 39), positive for any \hat Z>0. With \hat Z=0.1 the eigenvalue stays small but positive along the way, and the run is just slow.

Positive is a local guarantee

If \Lhp\vL>0 at the goal, IBVS converges from a neighbourhood of the goal; how large that neighbourhood is, the theorem does not say (in practice it is often large). Far from the goal, a positive eigenvalue only says that \norm{\ve'} shrinks at that instant, and even that exactly only for a constant \Lhp.

Try: π rad about z with \vL(\vs) and true Z. The eigenvalue is exactly 1 all along, yet the camera is lost to infinity and the dashed \norm{\ve'} grows: as the camera retreats, \Lhp changes so fast that the term neglected on slide 39 dominates.

The unreachable target of the next section is a second case: the eigenvalue stays at 1 and \ve'\to\mathbf 0, yet the camera stops with \ve\neq\mathbf 0. Far from the goal, \ve'=\mathbf 0 does not imply \ve=\mathbf 0.

An unreachable target is not a local minimum

Try: Make the target unreachable, or drag desired point 1, even slightly. Four points give 8 numbers, but the camera has only 6 degrees of freedom: the images it can produce form a 6-dimensional family, and a desired image edited by hand almost never belongs to it. No pose gives \ve=\mathbf 0.

IBVS then does the best it can. It moves until \ve'=\mathbf 0 and stops with \ve\neq\mathbf 0: the remaining error lies in \Null(\Lhp), outside \Range(\vL) (on the plot, the dashed \norm{\ve'} goes to zero while \norm{\ve} stays). The readouts show that this final error equals the smallest error any pose can reach: the camera stops at the best fit, spread over the four points. This is not a local minimum.

A local minimum is different: the target is reachable (some pose gives \ve=\mathbf 0), but the camera gets stuck elsewhere with \ve\neq\mathbf 0. For four points in a square these are rare (we found none in several thousand random starts); Chaumette and Hutchinson show one in their Fig. 9. In practice \vs^{*} is recorded from an image taken at the goal, so it is reachable by construction.
