Three points are not enough

The image of three points fixes only the directions of the three viewing rays, hence the angles between them. Finding the camera poses that see a known triangle under those angles is the perspective-3-point problem (P3P): it has up to four solutions.

The left view shows them for the triangle of slide 30, with pose 1 (the desired one) 1 m above its centre. Seen from any solution, points 1–3 fall on exactly the same pixels (difference around 10^{-15}).

Try: select pose 2: it is 25 cm above the table, beyond point 1, looking back. Then move pose 1 with the sliders: the solutions move, and their number can change.

What IBVS does with three points

With three points, \vs\in\R^6 and \vL is 6\times6. The error is zero at every P3P solution, so IBVS can stop at a wrong pose with \ve=\mathbf 0.

Try: select a solution other than pose 1 and press Play. Both runs start near that solution. With 3 points (orange) the camera settles on the wrong pose, with zero error. With 4 points (green) it goes to pose 1.

A fourth point

Point 4, at the centre of the triangle, is seen at a different place from every wrong solution (the red ring in the image). Adding it to the features removes the ambiguity. This is why IBVS uses at least four points, as on the next slides.
