From the image plane to pixels

The image plane of a digital camera is a W\times H grid of pixels of size \rho_w\times\rho_h, with the origin at the top-left corner. A point (x,y) of the image plane (in metres) has pixel coordinates

u=\frac{x}{\rho_w}+u_0,\qquad v=\frac{y}{\rho_h}+v_0,

where (u_0,v_0), the principal point, is where the optical axis meets the image. It is close to, but in general not exactly at, the centre of the image (the small cross).

Intrinsic parameters

With the projection, the intrinsic matrix collects these parameters:

\vK=\begin{bmatrix}f/\rho_w&0&u_0\\0&f/\rho_h&v_0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
\tilde{\vp}=\vK[\vI|\mathbf 0]\,\tilde{\vP}_C

Try: the focal length and the pixel size only appear as f/\rho_w and f/\rho_h: doubling both gives the same image. With \rho_w\neq\rho_h (non-square pixels) the image is stretched. Moving (u_0,v_0) shifts the whole image.

Camera matrix and homography

Intrinsics, projection and extrinsics combine into the 3\times4 camera matrix, from world points to pixels:

\tilde{\vp}=\vK[\vI|\mathbf 0]\,{}^C\vT_0\,\tilde{\vP}_0=\vC\,\tilde{\vP}_0 .

Since u=u'/w', \vC and \lambda\vC give the same pixels: \vC is known up to scale and has 11 unknowns.

Try: move the camera (azimuth, elevation, distance): \vK does not change, while the pose {}^C\vT_0, and with it \vC and \vH, does. Change f or (u_0,v_0) instead: {}^C\vT_0 stays the same.

For points on a plane, with the world z axis perpendicular to it (here the table, Z=0), the third column of \vC drops out and \vC becomes the 3\times3 homography \vH, with 8 unknowns: at least 4 point correspondences determine it. The table grid in the image is drawn with \vH.
