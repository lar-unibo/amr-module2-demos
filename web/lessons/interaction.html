From camera motion to image motion

A static point \vP=(X,Y,Z), in the frame of a camera that moves with \vv_c=(\nu_c,\omega_c), has velocity \dot{\vP}=-\nu_c-\omega_c\times\vP. Its normalized image coordinates x=X/Z, y=Y/Z then move as

\begin{aligned} \dot{\vs}=\vL_p\,\vv_c &=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}-\frac1Z&0&\frac xZ\\[2pt]0&-\frac1Z&\frac yZ\end{bmatrix}}_{\text{translation}}\nu_c\\[4pt] &+\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}xy&-(1+x^2)&y\\[2pt]1+y^2&-xy&-x\end{bmatrix}}_{\text{rotation}}\omega_c \end{aligned}

The left view shows the camera, the point, and the camera after a time T at constant velocity; the right view shows what the camera sees.

The depth matters only for translation

The first three columns, for \nu_c, scale with 1/Z: far points move less in the image. The last three, for \omega_c, do not depend on Z at all. This is why IBVS needs an estimate of the depth, and why a wrong estimate mostly changes the speed of the translation.

Try: choose sideways, then move the depth slider. Predict what happens to the orange arrow, then check. Repeat with pan: what happens to the violet arrow?

Invisible camera motions

\vL_p is 2\times6 with rank 2, so it has a 4-dimensional null space: camera motions that, to first order, do not move the point. Two of them are easy to picture: translating along the projection ray, and rotating about it. Both keep the point exactly still, for any duration.

Try: along the ray and about the ray, then a random motion in \Null(\vL_p). The random one starts with \dot{\vs}=0, but the point slowly drifts: \vL_p changes as the camera moves, so the null space is only an instantaneous property. Increase T to see the drift grow (it starts quadratically in time).

With several points, the null spaces intersect. Four points in general position leave no invisible motion, which is why IBVS uses at least four.

A first-order model

The dashed line is the prediction \vs+\dot{\vs}\,t with \vL_p frozen at t=0. It is exact for a sideways translation, where Z stays constant, but a forward motion or a rotation bends the true path away from it as T grows.

A visual servo does not rely on a long prediction: it measures the features again at every frame and recomputes \vL_p, so only the short-term prediction matters.
