Perspective projection

A camera turns the 3-D world into a 2-D image. With the image plane at z=f and the camera frame \{C\} with z along the optical axis, similar triangles give

x=f\,\frac{X}{Z},\qquad y=f\,\frac{Y}{Z}

or, in homogeneous coordinates,

\tilde{\vp}=\begin{bmatrix}f&0&0&0\\0&f&0&0\\0&0&1&0\end{bmatrix}\tilde{\vP}_C .

With f=1 the coordinates are the normalized image coordinates.

Extrinsics

The camera has an arbitrary pose with respect to the world frame \{0\}. A world point is first expressed in the camera frame, \tilde{\vP}_C={}^C\vT_0\,\tilde{\vP}_0: {}^C\vT_0 contains the extrinsic parameters (shown on the right). Move the camera and watch it change.

Try: change the focal length. The image grows and the field of view shrinks, as when zooming; the extrinsics do not change.

The depth is lost

Every point of a projection ray lands on the same image point.

Try: move the selected point along its ray (red, dashed): it moves in 3-D, not in the image. Then scale the whole scene about the camera: a house twice as big and twice as far away gives exactly the same image. From one image alone we cannot tell size from distance.

Thin lenses

A lens gathers more light than a pinhole. A thin lens forms the image of an object at distance z_o at distance z_i, with \frac{1}{z_o}+\frac{1}{z_i}=\frac{1}{f} (bottom cards). The image is where the rays from the tip of the object meet again: the ray parallel to the axis bends through F', the ray through the centre goes straight, the ray through F leaves parallel.

Try: start just above f: the image is far behind the lens and large. At z_o=2f it is at 2F', same size. Move the object away: the image moves to F' and shrinks (z_i\to f, right plot). With f=4 mm, an object at 0.5 m gives z_i-f\approx 0.03 mm, so the image plane is practically at z=f: the central-projection model. Below f the rays diverge and there is no real image.
