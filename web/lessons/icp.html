Correspondences are unknown

A depth camera sees the bracket on the table. Each measured pixel becomes a point s_j^c=d\,K^{-1}[u\;v\;1]^T, and with the known camera pose s_j=T_{cam}\,s_j^c (blue). Only the side facing the camera is measured; grazing surfaces give no depth (grey pixels in the depth image). Nobody tells us which model point m_i each s_j is.

ICP starts from an initial guess of T_{obj} and alternates two steps (slide 46):

  1. correspondences: for each scene point, the closest model point m_{c(j)}, with the model at the current pose (red lines);
  2. alignment: (R,t) in closed form from the pairs, as with known correspondences;

until the error stops changing:

E=\sum_j\lVert R\,m_{c(j)}+t-s_j\rVert^2 .

Try: press good guess (10° and 1.8 cm off) and watch the replay. The pairs are wrong at first, but each alignment improves them. After 26 iterations the pose is within 0.3° and 1 mm.

ICP is local

ICP only goes downhill from where it starts. A poor guess leads to a local minimum: a wrong pose where the closest points no longer pull the model anywhere.

Try: press bad guess (the bracket turned by 90°). ICP converges in 25 iterations to a pose 93° away. Its RMS of 19.7 mm is much higher than the 4.8 mm at the true pose, but ICP cannot know that. Move the rotation error slider to see how far the good basin reaches: from about −80° to +100° here. In practice a global, coarse method gives the guess first: markers, the previous frame, or RANSAC on feature matches. ICP then refines it.

Outliers and rejection

Real depth images contain stray measurements. Each one is paired with some model point and pulls the alignment towards it.

Try: outliers adds 120 stray depths: ICP converges 6° and 7 mm off. outliers + rejection drops every pair longer than 1.5 cm before the alignment (dashed grey lines), and the error is back to 0.3°. Rejecting the worst 15% works too. A tight threshold needs a good initial guess, otherwise it rejects the right pairs as well.

Point-to-plane

Point-to-point pairs a scene point with a sampled model point, so on flat faces the two clouds keep tugging sideways and many iterations are spent. Point-to-plane counts only the distance along the scene normal n_j, estimated by local PCA:

E=\sum_j\big((R\,m_{c(j)}+t-s_j)\cdot n_j\big)^2 .

There is no closed form. For a small rotation R\approx I+[\omega]_\times it becomes a linear least-squares problem in (\omega,t) at every iteration.

Try: point-to-plane, from the same guess: 14 iterations instead of 26. It slides the faces onto each other only if the camera sees faces in enough directions. Here it sees the top and two sides.

Slides and simulation

Lecture 3, slides 37 and 45–50 (Besl and McKay 1992; Chen and Medioni 1992; Rusinkiewicz and Levoy 2001). The bracket is 16 × 10 × 12 cm, with 1000 model points on its surface. The depth camera is 80 × 60 pixels with f=95 pixels, about 50 cm from the object. One ray per pixel finds the first surface. Depths have Gaussian noise, and rays meeting a surface at more than 83° from its normal are dropped. Outliers are random depths at random pixels around the object. The object is assumed segmented from the table.

Closest points are found by brute force (no k-d tree). The normals use the 10 nearest scene points. ICP stops when E changes by less than 10^{-4} (relative), or the pose moves by less than 0.02° and 0.02 mm, or after 60 iterations. The RMS of the pairs stays near 5 mm even at the true pose: the model is sampled every few millimetres. The initial guess turns the true pose about the vertical through the object frame and shifts it on the table.
